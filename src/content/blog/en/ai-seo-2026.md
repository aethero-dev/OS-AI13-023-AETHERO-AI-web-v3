## The turning point is here: the answer arrives before the click

The number of AI users is changing dramatically — and AI is in turn radically changing how people search. The figures below come from research covering Q3 and Q4 2025, and the curve is climbing steeply:

- alternative AI search engines (ChatGPT Search, Perplexity) are already used by **hundreds of millions of users**,
- **78 %** of companies already actively use AI,
- Google AI Overviews appeared in **42.51 %** of all searches in the study,
- when an AI-generated summary appears, clicks on traditional organic results drop sharply — Seer's analysis reports **50–65 % lower CTR** for results with an AI Overview,
- for paid CTR (PPC), queries with AI Overviews show a **68 %** drop.

Zero-click search is becoming the norm. The answer sits in the results — and the website serves as a source, not necessarily as a destination.

For an e-shop that doesn't mean the end of SEO. It means a new goal for SEO: instead of the click to your site, what matters is overall visibility and how often language models recommend you.

## The new goal of SEO: be cited, not just visited

Alongside classic SEO, the concept of **Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)** is taking hold.

Where traditional SEO deals with:

- rankings,
- clicks,
- traffic,

GEO deals with:

- whether AI uses your content,
- whether it credits you as a source,
- in what context it mentions your brand.

Both research and practice show that AI answers favour clearly structured, contextually grounded content that works with concrete data, statistics and verifiable sources. E-E-A-T signals (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trustworthiness) are equally key — demonstrable expertise, experience and credibility of the author or brand. **Entity SEO** plays a growing role: working with clearly defined concepts and the relationships between them, instead of merely repeating keywords. Generic "SEO copy for the robot" and keyword stuffing, by contrast, reduce performance in both AI and search over the long run.

## AI SEO isn't about tools. It's about what the content looks like

AI tools (including Shopify Magic) speed up the work — but on their own they won't secure visibility. What decides it is the form and structure of the content.

### How AI "reads" an e-shop

AI models aren't looking for creative prose. They look for:

- direct answers,
- clear definitions,
- comparisons,
- structured information,
- trustworthy context.

That's why content works today when it answers right at the start (the first 40–80 words), uses clear headings and lists, separates facts from marketing claims, and still makes sense when lifted out of the page's context.

## Product and collection pages as a source for AI

In the AI era a product page is no longer just a selling surface. It has to answer:

- what it is,
- who it's for,
- how it differs,
- when it makes sense to use.

Likewise, collections aren't just product listings but thematic wholes that define an area, explain differences and build context. That's exactly the content that appears most often in AI overviews.

## Structured data: the language AI speaks

AI systems need structure. In practice, these come up most often:

- **Product** schema (prices, availability, ratings),
- **FAQPage** schema (questions and answers),
- **Article** schema (author, date, context, sources),
- **HowTo** schema (procedures).

Research shows that most pages cited by AI use some form of structured data.

## E-E-A-T in the AI era: trust is filter number one

AI answers aren't only about relevance, they're mainly about trust. The E-E-A-T framework (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) is key today:

- **Experience** — real experience with the product or topic,
- **Expertise** — technical accuracy,
- **Authoritativeness** — brand authority,
- **Trustworthiness** — transparency and credibility.

That's why sites stand a better chance in AI answers when they have a clearly defined brand, work with reviews, state the author and context, and publish consistent, up-to-date information.

## Zero-click isn't a problem. It's filter number two

More than half of all searches now end without a click. That doesn't mean SEO stops working. It means that:

- part of the value shifts from traffic to brand visibility,
- AI picks only a limited number of sources,
- whoever isn't among them effectively doesn't exist.

SEO thus shifts from a tactical channel to a strategic discipline.
