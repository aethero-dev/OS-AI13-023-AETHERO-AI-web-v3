## E-shops are great at optimizing the purchase process

Online retailers optimize every detail — button colors, shipping text, payment method order — to maximize conversions. However, when customers want to withdraw from a purchase, they face a frustrating search through FAQs, terms and conditions, PDFs, and support emails.

New regulations will require e-shops to provide a clear, online withdrawal process. Implementation must be complete by June 19, 2026, though the legislation remains in process — verify with legal counsel before going live.

## It's not about the button. It's about the entire process

The mandatory button represents more than adding a link. When customers click "Withdraw from Contract", several things must happen simultaneously:

- Find the correct order
- Select items
- Submit the request
- Receive confirmation
- Merchant knows what to do next

The button is just an entry point. Without a clear process behind it, you've merely done cosmetics. This is why starting with external apps isn't ideal — first examine Shopify's native capabilities.

## What Shopify offers natively

Shopify offers **Self-serve returns**, allowing customers to request returns directly through their account or order status page. Customers log in, select orders, items, and return reasons, while merchants see requests in their admin panel.

However, Self-serve returns alone don't automatically satisfy regulatory requirements — they're a solid technical foundation, but not a complete solution.

### Setup steps

**Step 1:** Navigate to Settings -> Customer accounts to enable Self-serve returns.

Note: Legacy customer accounts don't support this feature; migration to new Customer accounts may be necessary.

**Step 2:** Configure Settings -> Policies -> Return rules to establish withdrawal timelines (14, 30, 90 days, or custom periods). Align these with your terms and actual policies.

## Where to place the button?

The mandatory button cannot be hidden in obscure locations like terms and conditions alone.

Create a dedicated "Returns and Withdrawal" page with a clear "Withdraw from Contract" button linking to the customer accounts URL. Include the same link in your footer and help center.

**Testing checklist:**

- Button is mobile-visible
- Language clearly indicates withdrawal intent
- Process works for test orders
- Confirmation email arrives
- Team knows where to find requests in admin

## Confirmation matters more than it seems

Legal experts emphasize that **the process must include withdrawal confirmation**.

Review Settings -> Notifications -> Returns. Customers should know upon submission that you've received their request, what it concerns, and what happens next. Have legal counsel review confirmation email language if it serves as official documentation.

## When native Shopify options may not suffice

Self-serve returns work well for standard physical goods but may not cover:

- Digital products
- Services and subscriptions
- Personalized items
- Products with withdrawal exemptions

Verify how the process handles unprocessed, partially processed, or unusual orders. Implementation requires combining settings, copy, UX design, and operational procedures.

## What to do now?

Don't wait until the deadline:

1. Enable Self-serve returns in test mode
2. Configure Return rules
3. Create a withdrawal page
4. Add a clear button
5. Test the entire process as a customer
6. Only then determine if custom templates, legal review, or additional apps are needed

Shopify provides the foundation. Responsibility for ensuring customers find a clear withdrawal path remains yours.
